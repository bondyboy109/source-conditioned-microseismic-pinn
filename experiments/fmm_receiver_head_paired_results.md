# Paired receiver-head training: learned versus FMM travel times

This is a **development-only waveform experiment**, not a source-localization test or an independent validation. The two 5 Hz waveform heads start from identical parameters and share the same frozen learned travel-time network, 29 FD training records, sampled receiver batches, and 5,000 Adam updates (seed 42). Only the travel time supplied to the head changes: the learned carrier for one head, or reciprocal FMM receiver fields for the other. Training uses receiver-record supervision only; it does not enforce PDE, initial-condition, or boundary-condition losses.

The evaluation uses 12 previously inspected FD development records, each displaced by 240 m along one of x, y, or z from one of two training anchors. Evaluation supplies each record's **true source** to the forward model; it does not estimate the source. These records were not used to train either head but are already development data, not untouched test data.

Full-record waveform error is `||prediction - FD||_2 / ||FD||_2` across all 25 receivers and 1,001 samples. Active-record error uses samples where `|FD|` is at least 5% of the record's maximum absolute FD amplitude. Response error compares the predicted *change from the corresponding anchor* to the FD change, divided by the FD-change norm; the response cosine measures directional agreement (higher is better). The table reports arithmetic means over development events, four per axis.

|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| All 12 | 0.508 | 0.540 | 0.436 | 0.467 | 0.430 | 0.459 | 0.864 | 0.867 |
| x (4) | 0.306 | 0.321 | 0.241 | 0.258 | 0.283 | 0.314 | 0.962 | 0.953 |
| y (4) | 0.350 | 0.366 | 0.282 | 0.301 | 0.264 | 0.284 | 0.967 | 0.962 |
| z (4) | 0.869 | 0.932 | 0.785 | 0.842 | 0.744 | 0.778 | 0.662 | 0.687 |

On the 29 training records, median full-record L2 is 0.194 (learned) versus 0.135 (FMM); mean L2 is 0.191 versus 0.135. The final sampled receiver loss before its 0.1 weight is 0.0965 versus 0.0578. Thus the FMM head fits the training records more closely, but its mean development full-record error is higher (0.540 versus 0.508), and it improves full-record L2 on only 5/12 development records. Its response L2 improves on only 2/12. Depth offsets remain the most difficult direction for both heads: their mean full-record L2 is 0.869 versus 0.932. The slightly higher FMM mean z response cosine does not reverse its higher response L2. This paired run offers no evidence that substituting FMM travel times alone improves unseen-source waveforms.

FMM used 80 m fields for 25 receivers, with up to 60 m receiver-coordinate snapping. Field construction took 37.24 s and cached 181.1 MiB; the two training runs together took 50.13 s on this run. These are setup and paired-training timings, not per-event inversion times or an FD/PINN break-even benchmark. The FMM field lookup is not differentiated with respect to continuous source coordinates. One seed, two anchors, one offset magnitude and already used development data limit the inference. No PDE-constrained training or new held-out localization experiment was performed; the previously reserved eight-event evaluation remains separate.

The next useful development check is the depth-response weakness: register several z offsets around the same anchors and assess waveform and first-arrival errors before choosing a source-differentiable travel-time representation. A future localization comparison needs a predeclared protocol and an untouched test set. Exact event-level results and input digests are in `fmm_receiver_head_paired_results.json`; the fixed settings are in `fmm_receiver_head_paired_protocol.json`. The two saved receiver heads are under `outputs/fmm_receiver_head_paired_5000/` (ignored by Git), and `examples/run_fmm_receiver_head_paired.py` refuses to overwrite this run.
