# Controlled physics-loss ablation on development events

This experiment compares the receiver-only objective with the same model
initialized from the same checkpoint and trained for the same 1,000 updates
with the PDE, initial-condition, and boundary-condition terms enabled. Both
runs use the 16 registered 5-Hz training events, seed 42, a 1e-5 learning
rate, batch size 1024, all 16 events per batch, receiver-loss weight 0.1,
the 20 m travel-time distance core, and the same model architecture. The
three development events at 250, 800, and 2000 m remain evaluation-only.

Receiver mini-batches are generated by a private NumPy/Torch RNG seeded with
42. This prevents the extra random draws used by PDE/IC/BC sampling from
changing the receiver batches in the hybrid run. The pairing test is in
`tests/test_receiver_sampling.py`.

| Objective | 250 m raw L2 | 800 m raw L2 | 2000 m raw L2 | 250 m shift+gain L2 | 800 m shift+gain L2 | 2000 m shift+gain L2 | wall time | peak CUDA memory |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Receiver-only | 0.7695 | 0.9999 | 0.9287 | 0.6722 | 0.8519 | 0.8873 | 4.65 s | 31.9 MiB |
| Receiver + PDE/IC/BC | 0.7555 | 0.9924 | 0.9355 | 0.6600 | 0.8439 | 0.8909 | 47.31 s | 112.8 MiB |

The hybrid objective reduces raw relative L2 by 0.0140 and 0.0075 at 250 and
800 m, respectively, but increases it by 0.0068 at 2000 m. The differences
are small relative to the residual error and do not establish a robust
cross-source improvement. Both objectives retain substantial late-coda error:

| Event | Objective | 0.5--1.0 s error energy / full energy | 1.0--2.0 s error energy / full energy | 1.0--2.0 s relative L2 |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 250 m | Receiver-only | 0.1112 | 0.4779 | 0.9612 |
| 250 m | Hybrid | 0.1048 | 0.4636 | 0.9466 |
| 800 m | Receiver-only | 0.1336 | 0.8101 | 1.2229 |
| 800 m | Hybrid | 0.1155 | 0.8126 | 1.2248 |
| 2000 m | Receiver-only | 0.1623 | 0.5306 | 1.0506 |
| 2000 m | Hybrid | 0.1524 | 0.5423 | 1.0621 |

The physical objective therefore slightly improves the 250 m early/coda
diagnostics and the 800 m 0.5--1.0 s interval, but does not remove the
late-coda mismatch and is marginally worse at 2000 m. It is not justified to
claim that PDE regularization solves source-displacement generalization.
The computational cost is material: under this small development setting,
hybrid training takes about 10.2 times longer and uses about 3.5 times more
peak GPU memory than receiver-only training.

Reproduce the two runs with the commands below (PowerShell):

```powershell
$obs = Get-ChildItem .\data\observations\window_0to2s\train_*_0to2s.npz | ForEach-Object FullName
python .\train_source_conditioned_pinn.py --travel-time-carrier --distance-core-m 20 --wavefield-scale 1e-5 --initial-checkpoint .\outputs\overthrust_core20_train16_jointbatch_receiver_5hz_5000\conditioned_5Hz.pth --frequencies 5 --epochs 1000 --save-every 1000 --learning-rate 1e-5 --n-phys 256 --n-ic 128 --n-bc-per-face 16 --receiver-batch-size 1024 --receiver-events-per-batch 16 --receiver-loss-weight 0.1 --receiver-observations $obs --receiver-only --output .\outputs\controlled_physics_ablation_receiver_1000
python .\train_source_conditioned_pinn.py --travel-time-carrier --distance-core-m 20 --wavefield-scale 1e-5 --initial-checkpoint .\outputs\overthrust_core20_train16_jointbatch_receiver_5hz_5000\conditioned_5Hz.pth --frequencies 5 --epochs 1000 --save-every 1000 --learning-rate 1e-5 --n-phys 256 --n-ic 128 --n-bc-per-face 16 --receiver-batch-size 1024 --receiver-events-per-batch 16 --receiver-loss-weight 0.1 --receiver-observations $obs --output .\outputs\controlled_physics_ablation_hybrid_1000
```
